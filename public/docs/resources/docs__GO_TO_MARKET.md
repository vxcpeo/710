# SONIRA — Go-to-Market and 90-Day Operating Playbook

**Prepared 17 September 2026 · Version 2.0.** All customer-facing copy is English. V2 local contract, fork, browser and production-build validation are complete; public testnet deployment is pending. This is an execution plan, not evidence that registration, outreach, public deployment, publication or campaigns occurred.

## 1. One audience, one promise, one action

Start with English-speaking independent beat makers, original sound designers and small wallet-native communities. Prefer creators with a recognizable sound and a recurring community use over follower count.

**Core promise:** “Make your sound a community.”

**Product explanation:** “Publish a short original sound as a playable onchain token. Listen, inspect the rules and explore a market that can graduate to locked liquidity on Robinhood Chain testnet.”

**Primary action:** “Launch a sound.” **Secondary action:** “Explore sounds.”

Show “Testnet” near prelaunch actions. Explain creator economics as a 1% base fee, split 70% creator / 30% protocol in both phases, plus any creator-selected tax. Optional buybacks purchase that sound token for linear vesting; they do not burn a platform token or guarantee price support. Never imply token ownership grants music copyright, royalties, company equity, audited safety or a Robinhood partnership.

The selected brand is SONIRA, with `sonira.xyz` and `@sonira`. [Public checks](research__brand-verification.md) found no registered domain/public profile at the recorded time; neither has been acquired. Registrar control and successful X signup are required before advertising operated destinations. Public search absence is not trademark clearance or a signup guarantee.

## 3. Creator cohort design

Recruit 15 interview participants before choosing the first cohort. Ask about their last original sound, how it was shared, how they recognized returning listeners, and what their community did afterward. Record behavior, not polite reactions to a pitch.

Select 10–15 design partners who have rights to a short clip, an identifiable community use, a testable EVM wallet and willingness to give candid feedback. Document criteria and distinguish compensated research participants from organic customers.

1. Explain permanent public media, creator rights obligations and what a sound token does not convey.
2. Upload WAV/MP3/M4A/OGG/WebM or record up to 35 seconds. Preview the prepared audio and optional JPEG cover. Audio plus cover must fit 98,304 bytes; cover alone is capped at 16,384 bytes. Full-length output needs usable Opus support; PCM fallback supports about 12 seconds without a cover.
3. Review immutable metadata, quote asset, fee recipient, creator tax and buyback choice. Explain the 0.0005 test ETH launch fee plus gas. Only native ETH and the five verified stock-named test assets are initially enabled; their names do not imply real stock ownership.
4. Confirm the launch-window charge and declared exemptions. The 99% nominal / 15-second anti-snipe setting is capped at 98% with 1% base fee and zero creator tax. Never market this as a guaranteed fair or bot-free launch.
5. Sign the atomic studio publication, verify its receipt and replay reconstructed chain media before sharing.
6. Invite existing community members voluntarily. Collect feedback at days 2, 7, 14 and 30, with no requirement to buy.
7. In isolated test markets, exercise direct curve seller proceeds/refunds, accrued fees, operator sweep, escrow claim, buyback release, two-phase graduation and deadline-bounded v4 swaps. Do not create trades merely to inflate public milestones.

Research compensation may be fixed for time or original commissioned assets. It must not depend on price, volume, recruiting buyers or fabricated engagement. Disclose paid content and obtain permission for case studies.

## 4. Channel strategy

| Channel | Initial use | Success measure | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | Original sound demos, mechanics and creator stories | Qualified visits, engaged plays and opted-in creators | Register first; organic before paid |
| Creator communities | Permission-based pilot invitations | Completed interviews and returning creators | No bulk unsolicited messaging |
| Chain developer ecosystem | Verified testnet work and useful integration lessons | Builder referrals and real conversations | No partnership claim without agreement |
| Shareable sound pages | Let creators share a playable object | Listener return and attributable activation | Advertise embeds only after implemented |
| Creator collaborations | Commission original short sounds | Publishing success and D14 return | Fixed fees and explicit rights |
| Educational/search content | Explain publication, fees and claims | Visits completing useful actions | No income promises or unsupported superlatives |

The first 90-day research/acquisition ceiling is **$6,000**, within the $36,000 year-one budget: up to $2,000 interviews/usability, $2,000 original commissions, $1,000 transparent distribution tests and $1,000 production/accessibility contingencies. Later quarterly ceilings are $8,000, $10,000 and $12,000, released only with cohort and contribution evidence. A ceiling is not a requirement to spend. Respect advertising restrictions and eligible jurisdictions.

## 6. Content system

| Pillar | Content | Required proof |
|---|---|---|
| Sound and creators | Play a sound, show how it was made and explain its community use | Original/licensed material and creator permission |
| Product clarity | One action: publish, inspect, trade, sweep, claim, vest or graduate | Current working flow and accurate phase-specific mechanics |
| Building in public | A verified receipt, usability improvement or measured lesson | Actual code/transaction or consented evidence |

The three introductory social posts should establish: **the sound-first concept; the creator fee and claim mechanics; the transparent testnet lifecycle**. Prepared JPGs and copy remain unpublished until the real X account is controlled and release facts are checked. Refresh every economic claim against v2 before use: older 50/50 curve-only sharing and inaccessible 0.3% LP income descriptions are obsolete.

Use “fixed initial supply of one billion” rather than implying burns cannot change total supply. Describe the 1% base fee as continuing through graduation, with 70% creator / 30% protocol. Extra creator tax is separate and entirely for the creator. If discussing buyback, disclose that half of the creator base-fee bucket funds it, execution is conditional, tokens vest linearly over five years, and released tokens split 70/30 through escrow. A five-year cliff or platform-token burn would be incorrect.

Every transaction explainer includes the relevant amount/asset, fees, minimum output and material conditions. Graduated pool fees use the unspecified currency amount, normally the output for exact-input swaps; do not simplify this into a guaranteed percentage of input notional. Present the two graduation stages and real administrative recovery powers plainly. Testnet balances are not revenue, customer deposits or investment performance.

## 7. Funnel, definitions and dashboard

| Stage | Definition | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified visit | Human visit from a relevant creator/community source | Source-attributed visits; exclude obvious bots |
| Engaged listener | Intentional play reaches five seconds or half the clip | Engaged plays and seven-day listener return |
| Activated creator | Successful original-content publication after review | Completion rate and time excluding funding |
| Activated wallet | Confirmed voluntary first eligible trade | Quote-to-confirm rate, failures and fee comprehension |
| Retained creator | Publishes again or maintains measured community activity | D14/D30 return with denominator and cutoff |
| Monetized activity | Legitimate fee-bearing curve/pool executions | Fee-basis value and actual protocol claims by asset |
| Working payout | Accrual reconciles through sweep/escrow to claim | Sweep age, claim success and support burden |

Report D14 using days 12–16 and D30 using days 27–33 after first publication. Include cohort size, eligible sample, numerator, denominator and observation date. Wallets are not unique people; flag suspicious links and obvious self-trading while admitting detection limits.

The weekly dashboard tracks new/returning creators, relaunches, engaged plays, wallet/codec errors, publication completion, curve/pool mix, fee basis, accrued/swept/claimed fees, vested/released tokens, graduation delays, operator failures, support hours and cash spent. Preserve each asset's units. Do not count unsold sound-token vesting as company cash, add creator tax to protocol revenue or include anti-snipe windfalls in recurring forecasts. Internal QA and sample content stay outside live growth totals.

## 8. Experiment cards

**A — Does the sound explain the value?** Compare two clear landing explanations with a functioning player and the same audience. Measure engaged play followed by voluntary exploration. Keep allocation and sample sizes visible; a few sessions do not establish statistical significance.

**B — Can a creator publish alone?** Observe at least 10 qualified creators using their own clip. Record codec, size, funding, wallet and fee-setting confusion. Target 70% unassisted completion and a correct explanation of irreversible publication. Failure triggers product work before promotion.

**C — Does a community return?** Run two cohorts of at least 20 creators where possible, without trading rewards. Measure D14/D30 creator use and repeat listening. Below 15% D30 across both cohorts triggers a segment/product revision; incomplete windows yield no conclusion.

**D — Are money flows understandable?** Show a curve buy/sell, a fee sweep/claim, a graduated swap and a vest release. Ask users to explain the 1% base fee, 70/30 split, extra tax, operator dependency and linear vesting. They should distinguish direct seller output, unswept accrual, escrow claims and vested sound tokens. Include buyback-off and buyback-on examples, actual anti-snipe exemptions, and the waiting stage before pool creation. Target 80% accurate responses.

**E — Is acquisition economical?** After retention evidence, run a small attributed test. Model company quote revenue at 0.3% of fee-basis volume across both phases, less measured incremental costs. The current illustration gives $2.70 monthly contribution per wallet at $1,000 fee basis; six active months and $15 CAC yield only 1.08× LTV/CAC. Scale only with observed contribution LTV/CAC above 3× and payback under six active months. Testnet volume cannot establish real-money LTV.

**F — Can fee operations sustain the promise?** Simulate operator downtime, a stale minimum, a paused quote asset, vest top-ups and a delayed graduation. Measure detection, diagnosis, retry and reconciliation time. A successful claim is the outcome; a submitted sweep alone is not. Never weaken minimum-output checks to meet a service target.

## 9. Operational cadence and response

Hold a weekly product/creator review and monthly cash/security review. Engineering owns reserves, fee ledgers and operator health; operations owns creator support and rights intake; the founder releases budget and checks claims. Assign one owner and next action to every issue.

Daily fee workflow: read current curve/hook balances and asset state; separate direct payout from required conversion/buyback; obtain a fresh executable quote; set nonzero minimum outputs under the configured movement bound; simulate; submit through the authorized operator where required; wait for receipts; reconcile accrual reductions, escrow credits and vest deposits; and alert/retry with new quotes after recoverable failures. Creator direct sweeps are available only where no internal swap is needed. Investigate eligible balances unswept for more than 24 hours during the pilot and publish service targets only after measurement.

Curve buybacks may fall back to creator payout when inventory or movement bounds prevent execution; the graduation sweep skips its buyback. Pool conversions can be necessary before fees become quote-asset claims. Neither is an invisible accounting detail: show actual outcomes. A beneficiary's vest release credits both shares into escrow, then each claims its balance. Top-ups preserve already vested amounts and weight remaining duration rather than resetting everything to a five-year cliff.

For incidents, preserve evidence, warn users on controlled surfaces, stop recommending affected actions, evaluate direct-contract exposure, engage specialists and publish verified updates. Owner capabilities include future configuration/allowlists, operator rotation, buyback disable, a three-day delayed recipient override executable within the following three days, and seven-day swept-reserve rescue. These powers create trust risk as well as recovery options. External issuer pauses, blocklists, upgrades and burns can still prevent settlement. Do not promise a universal pause or guaranteed recovery.

For content reports, acknowledge through the monitored intake, review evidence, restrict operated listings when justified, notify parties where lawful and permit an appeal. Explain that delisting does not erase immutable chain data. Avoid republishing disputed media in reports.

## 10. Expansion and stop decisions

Add creators when publishing is reliable, fees reconcile and a returning cohort is visible. Commercial launch requires independent review of the modified contracts, remediated findings, verified deployment, legal/jurisdiction decisions, monitored operator/treasury controls, rights operations and complete disclosures. Real stock products require a separate issuer, eligibility, liquidity and legal workstream. USDG6/cbBTC8 architecture and 66 mainnet reference assets do not establish equivalent testnet listings or commercial approval.

Expand audio/cover compatibility, embeds and creator tools when measured friction and retention justify the work. No separate SONIRA platform token is part of the plan. Optional sound-token buyback vesting is not evidence that a new platform-token economy is needed.

Rework the segment below 15% D30 creator retention in both measured cohorts. Pause campaigns below 3× contribution LTV/CAC or above six months' payback. Suspend expansion for critical findings, reserve/escrow discrepancies, sustained operator failure or missing rights response. Reduce burn at less than six months' company runway. User reserves and creator claims never finance the business.
